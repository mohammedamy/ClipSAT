14 Statistics & Probability Foundational [1 mark]
The bar graph below shows the distribution of the number of siblings reported by the 25 students in a homeroom class.

If 1 student is selected at random from the class, what is the probability that the selected student has at least 2 siblings?
A. 625\frac{6}{25}256​
B. 925\frac{9}{25}259​
C. 1625\frac{16}{25}2516​
D. 1225\frac{12}{25}2512​
Correct Answer: (B) — 925\frac{9}{25}259​
To find the probability that a randomly chosen student has at least 2 siblings:

1. Find the total number of outcomes (students):
6+10+6+3=256 + 10 + 6 + 3 = 256+10+6+3=25

2. Find the number of favorable outcomes. The phrase "at least 2 siblings" means having 2 or more siblings (either 2 or 3 siblings):
- Number of students with 2 siblings: 666
- Number of students with 3 siblings: 333
- Favorable outcomes: 6+3=96 + 3 = 96+3=9

3. Calculate the probability:
P(at least 2 siblings)=925P(\text{at least 2 siblings}) = \frac{9}{25}P(at least 2 siblings)=259​

Common Misconceptions:
- 625\frac{6}{25}256​ counts only students with exactly 2 siblings, failing to include those with 3.
- 1625\frac{16}{25}2516​ incorrectly counts students with fewer than 2 siblings (0 or 1 sibling: 6+10=166 + 10 = 166+10=16).
