18 Probability, Conditional Probability & Two-Way Tables Foundational [1 mark]
The table below shows the number of students in a school who play soccer or basketball, and whether they are in 9th grade or 10th grade.

What is the probability that a randomly selected student is a 10th grader, given that they play basketball?
Official SPR Answer [1 point] — Verified Grid-In Value: 35\frac{3}{5}53​
Method & Working: Let B be the event that a student plays basketball, and let T be the event that a student is in 10th grade. We are looking for the conditional probability P(T|B), which can be calculated as P(T and B) / P(B).

From the table, the number of students who are in 10th grade and play basketball is 45.

The total number of students who play basketball is 75.

Therefore, the probability that a randomly selected student plays basketball is 75/180.

The probability that a randomly selected student is a 10th grader and plays basketball is 45/180.

The conditional probability P(T|B) = P(T and B) / P(B) = (45/180) / (75/180) = 45/75.

Simplifying the fraction 45/75 by dividing both numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor, which is 15, we get 3/5.

Alternatively, we can directly use the information from the table. Given that a student plays basketball, we look at the 'Basketball' column. The total number of students who play basketball is 75. Out of these 75 students, 45 are in 10th grade. So, the probability is 45/75, which simplifies to 3/5. [M1][A1]
