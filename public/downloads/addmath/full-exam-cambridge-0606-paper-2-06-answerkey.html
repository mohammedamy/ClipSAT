1 Differentiation, Rules & Rates of Change [8 marks]
The diagram shows part of the curve y=ax3+bx2+9x+1y = a x^3 + b x^2 + 9x + 1y=ax3+bx2+9x+1, where aaa and bbb are constants. The curve has a stationary point at (1,5)(1, 5)(1,5).

Find the coordinates of the other stationary point on the curve. [8]
Official Examiner Mark Scheme [8 marks] — Final Answer: (3,1)(3, 1)(3,1)
Mark Scheme (CAIE / Edexcel Grading Steps):
- Step 1 (Method & Formulation): Identify the required geometric/algebraic relationship from the diagram and substitute the given values. [M1]
- Step 2 (Derivation & Calculation): To find the coordinates of the other stationary point, we first determine the unknown parameters aaa and bbb.

Step 1: Use the given coordinates of the point on the curve
Since the point (1,5)(1, 5)(1,5) lies on the curve:
5=a(1)3+b(1)2+9(1)+15 = a(1)^3 + b(1)^2 + 9(1) + 15=a(1)3+b(1)2+9(1)+1
5=a+b+10  ⟹  a+b=−5— (Equation 1)5 = a + b + 10 \implies a + b = -5 \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}5=a+b+10⟹a+b=−5— (Equation 1)

Step 2: Differentiate to find dydx\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}dxdy​
dydx=3ax2+2bx+9\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 3a x^2 + 2b x + 9dxdy​=3ax2+2bx+9

Step 3: Apply the stationary condition at x=1x = 1x=1
At a stationary point, dydx=0\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 0dxdy​=0:
3a(1)2+2b(1)+9=0  ⟹  3a+2b=−9— (Equation 2)3a(1)^2 + 2b(1) + 9 = 0 \implies 3a + 2b = -9 \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}3a(1)2+2b(1)+9=0⟹3a+2b=−9— (Equation 2)

Step 4: Solve the simultaneous equations
Multiplying Equation 1 by 2:
2a+2b=−102a + 2b = -102a+2b=−10
Subtracting this from Equation 2:
(3a+2b)−(2a+2b)=−9−(−10)  ⟹  a=1(3a + 2b) - (2a + 2b) = -9 - (-10) \implies a = 1(3a+2b)−(2a+2b)=−9−(−10)⟹a=1
Substituting a=1a = 1a=1 into Equation 1:
1+b=−5  ⟹  b=−61 + b = -5 \implies b = -61+b=−5⟹b=−6

Step 5: Find the other stationary point
Substitute a=1a = 1a=1 and b=−6b = -6b=−6 into the derivative:
dydx=3x2−12x+9=0\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 3x^2 - 12x + 9 = 0dxdy​=3x2−12x+9=0
Dividing by 3:
x2−4x+3=0  ⟹  (x−1)(x−3)=0x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0 \implies (x - 1)(x - 3) = 0x2−4x+3=0⟹(x−1)(x−3)=0
Thus, the other stationary point occurs at x=3x = 3x=3.

Step 6: Calculate the corresponding yyy-coordinate
Substitute x=3x = 3x=3 back into the equation of the curve y=x3−6x2+9x+1y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 1y=x3−6x2+9x+1:
y=(3)3−6(3)2+9(3)+1=27−54+27+1=1y = (3)^3 - 6(3)^2 + 9(3) + 1 = 27 - 54 + 27 + 1 = 1y=(3)3−6(3)2+9(3)+1=27−54+27+1=1
Therefore, the other stationary point is (3,1)(3, 1)(3,1).

*Common Trap:* Substituting x=3x = 3x=3 into the derivative dydx\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}dxdy​ instead of the original curve equation yields y=0y = 0y=0, leading erroneously to (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0). [M1]
- Step 3 (Final Accuracy): Verified exact result: (3,1)(3, 1)(3,1). [A1]
