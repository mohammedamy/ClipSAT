∣2x−6∣=mx+2|2x - 6| = mx + 2∣2x−6∣=mx+2

We consider the function y=∣2x−6∣y = |2x - 6|y=∣2x−6∣ and the line y=mx+2y = mx + 2y=mx+2.

2. Analyze the line y=mx+2y = mx + 2y=mx+2:
The line has a fixed yyy-intercept at (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2) and variable gradient mmm.

3. Lower boundary (passing through the vertex):

0=m(3)+2  ⟹  m=−23.0 = m(3) + 2 \implies m = -\frac{2}{3}.0=m(3)+2⟹m=−32​.
- If m<−23m < -\frac{2}{3}m<−32​, the line passes below the vertex (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0) and does not intersect the right ray x≥3x \ge 3x≥3.
- At m=−23m = -\frac{2}{3}m=−32​, the line meets the vertex (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0) as its only intersection point on both rays, giving exactly one real solution.
- Therefore, for two distinct solutions, we must have m>−23m > -\frac{2}{3}m>−32​.

4. Upper boundary (parallelism with the right ray):
The right ray has gradient 222.
- If m=2m = 2m=2, the line y=2x+2y = 2x + 2y=2x+2 is parallel to y=2x−6y = 2x - 6y=2x−6 and never intersects the right ray; it intersects only the left ray at −2x+6=2x+2  ⟹  x=1-2x + 6 = 2x + 2 \implies x = 1−2x+6=2x+2⟹x=1 (1 solution).
