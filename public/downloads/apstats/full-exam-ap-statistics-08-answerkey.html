They surveyed a random sample of 500 residents and categorized them into three SES levels: Low, Medium, and High. Civic engagement was also categorized into three levels: None, Some, and High. The results are summarized in the two-way table below. A chi-square test for homogeneity is planned to investigate if the distribution of civic engagement levels differs across SES groups.

If the null hypothesis of homogeneity is rejected, which of the following statements best describes a potential conclusion from this test, assuming the observed counts are representative of the city's population?
A. Socioeconomic status has no impact on the distribution of civic engagement levels.
B. The distribution of civic engagement levels significantly differs across the socioeconomic status groups. ✓
C. The proportion of residents with high civic engagement is the same across all socioeconomic status levels.
D. There is no association between socioeconomic status and civic engagement.
Correct Answer: (B) — The distribution of civic engagement levels significantly differs across the socioeconomic status groups.
The chi-square test for homogeneity tests whether the distribution of a categorical variable (civic engagement) is the same across different groups (socioeconomic status levels). If the null hypothesis is rejected, it means there is sufficient evidence to conclude that the distribution of civic engagement levels is NOT the same across all SES groups. Statement A is too broad and refers to association, which is tested by a chi-square test of independence. While related, homogeneity focuses on the distribution across groups. Statement B is a restatement of the null hypothesis, which we are assuming is rejected. Statement C is a specific instance of the null hypothesis being true; rejecting the null means we have evidence against such specific claims (and more generally, against the null hypothesis of equal distributions). Therefore, statement D is the most appropriate conclusion when the null hypothesis of homogeneity is rejected.
