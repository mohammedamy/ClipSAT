19 Similarity Challenging [1 mark]
Which of the following conditions is NOT sufficient to prove that two triangles are similar?
A. Two pairs of corresponding angles are congruent (AA)
B. Three pairs of corresponding sides are proportional (SSS)
C. Two pairs of sides are proportional and a non-included angle is congruent (SSA)
D. Two pairs of sides are proportional and their included angle is congruent (SAS)
Correct Answer: (C) — Two pairs of sides are proportional and a non-included angle is congruent (SSA)
SSA (Side-Side-Angle) is NOT a valid similarity criterion. When the congruent angle is not included between the proportional sides, the third side can swing to form two non-congruent, non-similar triangles (the ambiguous case). In contrast, AA, SAS, and SSS all rigorously guarantee triangle similarity.
20 Probability Challenging [1 mark]
A quality control laboratory receives components sorted into two bins:
- Bin A contains 10 components, of which exactly 3 are defective and 7 are non-defective.
- Bin B contains 10 components, of which exactly 6 are defective and 4 are non-defective.

To conduct an inspection, an automated system first selects a bin at random: it rolls a fair standard six-sided die and selects Bin A if the result is 1 or 2; otherwise (if the result is 3, 4, 5, or 6), it selects Bin B. From the chosen bin, the system then randomly samples 2 components without replacement.

If both sampled components are found to be defective, what is the probability that the components were drawn from Bin A?
A. 19\dfrac{1}{9}91​
B. 1011\dfrac{10}{11}1110​
C. 111\dfrac{1}{11}111​
D. 16\dfrac{1}{6}61​
Correct Answer: (C) — 111\dfrac{1}{11}111​
We use Bayes' theorem combined with combinations to find the conditional probability P(Bin A∣both defective)P(\text{Bin A} \mid \text{both defective})P(Bin A∣both defective).

Step 1: Determine prior probabilities of selecting each bin.
The die has 6 equally likely outcomes:
- Bin A is selected if the die shows 1 or 2:
P(A)=26=13P(A) = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}P(A)=62​=31​
- Bin B is selected if the die shows 3, 4, 5, or 6:
P(B)=46=23P(B) = \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}P(B)=64​=32​

Step 2: Determine conditional probabilities of sampling 2 defective components without replacement.
Let D2D_2D2​ denote the event that both selected components are defective.
- From Bin A (3 defective out of 10):
P(D2∣A)=(32)(102)=345=115P(D_2 \mid A) = \dfrac{\binom{3}{2}}{\binom{10}{2}} = \dfrac{3}{45} = \dfrac{1}{15}P(D2​∣A)=(210​)(23​)​=453​=151​
- From Bin B (6 defective out of 10):
P(D2∣B)=(62)(102)=1545=13P(D_2 \mid B) = \dfrac{\binom{6}{2}}{\binom{10}{2}} = \dfrac{15}{45} = \dfrac{1}{3}P(D2​∣B)=(210​)(26​)​=4515​=31​

Step 3: Apply the Law of Total Probability to find P(D2)P(D_2)P(D2​).
P(D2)=P(A)⋅P(D2∣A)+P(B)⋅P(D2∣B)P(D_2) = P(A) \cdot P(D_2 \mid A) + P(B) \cdot P(D_2 \mid B)P(D2​)=P(A)⋅P(D2​∣A)+P(B)⋅P(D2​∣B)
P(D2)=(13)(345)+(23)(1545)=3135+30135=33135=1145P(D_2) = \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)\left(\dfrac{3}{45}\right) + \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)\left(\dfrac{15}{45}\right) = \dfrac{3}{135} + \dfrac{30}{135} = \dfrac{33}{135} = \dfrac{11}{45}P(D2​)=(31​)(453​)+(32​)(4515​)=1353​+13530​=13533​=4511​

Step 4: Apply Bayes' theorem.
P(A∣D2)=P(A)⋅P(D2∣A)P(D2)=313533135=333=111P(A \mid D_2) = \dfrac{P(A) \cdot P(D_2 \mid A)}{P(D_2)} = \dfrac{\dfrac{3}{135}}{\dfrac{33}{135}} = \dfrac{3}{33} = \dfrac{1}{11}P(A∣D2​)=P(D2​)P(A)⋅P(D2​∣A)​=13533​1353​​=333​=111​

Common Misconceptions:
- Choice B (19\frac{1}{9}91​) occurs if sampling is incorrectly modeled with replacement, giving likelihoods (310)2(\frac{3}{10})^2(103​)2 and (610)2(\frac{6}{10})^2(106​)2.
