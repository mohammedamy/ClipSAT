20 Foundations & Reasoning Challenging [1 mark]
Consider the following conditional statement where the universe of discourse is the set of all parallelograms:

Statement P: "If the diagonals of a parallelogram are perpendicular, then the parallelogram is a square."\text{Statement } P\text{: "If the diagonals of a parallelogram are perpendicular, then the parallelogram is a square."}Statement P: "If the diagonals of a parallelogram are perpendicular, then the parallelogram is a square."

Which of the following correctly classifies the truth values of Statement PPP, its converse, its inverse, and its contrapositive, along with a mathematically valid counterexample for any false statement(s)?
A. Statement PPP is False (counterexample: a rectangle with unequal adjacent sides);
Converse is True;
Inverse is True;
Contrapositive is False (counterexample: a rectangle with unequal adjacent sides).
B. Statement PPP is False (counterexample: a rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths);
Converse is True;
Inverse is True;
Contrapositive is False (counterexample: a rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths).
C. Statement PPP is False (counterexample: a rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths);
Converse is False (counterexample: a non-square rhombus);
Inverse is False (counterexample: a non-square rectangle);
Contrapositive is False (counterexample: a rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths).
D. Statement PPP is True;
Converse is False (counterexample: a rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths);
Inverse is False (counterexample: a rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths);
Contrapositive is True.
Correct Answer: (B) — Statement PPP is False (counterexample: a rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths);
Converse is True;
Inverse is True;
Contrapositive is False (counterexample: a rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths).
Let the universe of discourse be the set of all parallelograms. Define the component propositions:
- ppp: "The diagonals of the parallelogram are perpendicular."
- qqq: "The parallelogram is a square."

1. Statement PPP (Conditional p→qp \to qp→q):
- A conditional statement p→qp \to qp→q is false if and only if there exists a counterexample where the hypothesis ppp is true and the conclusion qqq is false.
- A rhombus with unequal diagonal lengths (a non-square rhombus) is a parallelogram whose diagonals are perpendicular (so ppp is true), but its four interior angles are not all 90∘90^\circ90∘, so it is not a square (thus qqq is false).
- Therefore, Statement PPP is False.
- *Misconception alert:* A non-square rectangle cannot serve as a counterexample to p→qp \to qp→q because its diagonals are not perpendicular (its hypothesis ppp is false).

2. Converse (q→pq \to pq→p):
- "If a parallelogram is a square, then its diagonals are perpendicular."
- Every square is a rhombus, and all rhombi have mutually perpendicular diagonals. Hence, this statement is True.

3. Inverse (¬p→¬q\neg p \to \neg q¬p→¬q):
- "If the diagonals of a parallelogram are not perpendicular, then the parallelogram is not a square."
- The inverse is logically equivalent to the converse (¬p→¬q≡q→p\neg p \to \neg q \equiv q \to p¬p→¬q≡q→p). Since the converse is true, the inverse is True.

4. Contrapositive (¬q→¬p\neg q \to \neg p¬q→¬p):
- "If a parallelogram is not a square, then its diagonals are not perpendicular."
- The contrapositive is logically equivalent to the original conditional (¬q→¬p≡p→q\neg q \to \neg p \equiv p \to q¬q→¬p≡p→q). Since Statement PPP is false, the contrapositive is False.
- A valid counterexample to ¬q→¬p\neg q \to \neg p¬q→¬p must make ¬q\neg q¬q true (not a square) and ¬p\neg p¬p false (diagonals are perpendicular). A rhombus with unequal diagonals satisfies both conditions.
