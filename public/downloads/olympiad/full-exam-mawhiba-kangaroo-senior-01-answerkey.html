10 One-Variable Statistics, Distributions & Margin of Error Challenging [1 mark]
A study finds a strong positive correlation between city population and number of car accidents. A researcher concludes that city population directly causes an increase in number of car accidents. What is the most likely flaw in this conclusion?
A. There may be a lurking (confounding) variable, such as overall traffic volume, that explains both ✓
B. The sample size must have been too large to detect the true relationship
C. Correlation of this strength always implies causation, so there is no flaw
D. The margin of error was not reported, which invalidates any correlation
Correct Answer: (A) — There may be a lurking (confounding) variable, such as overall traffic volume, that explains both
Correlation does not imply causation; a lurking variable like overall traffic volume could be driving both city population and number of car accidents.
