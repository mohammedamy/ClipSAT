14 Conditional Probability & Bayes' Rule Challenging [1 mark]
A medical test for a rare disease has a 99\% accuracy rate in detecting the disease when it is present (True Positive Rate) and a 95\% accuracy rate in identifying individuals without the disease (True Negative Rate). If the prevalence of the disease in the population is 0.5\%, what is the probability that a person who tests positive actually has the disease?
A. Approximately0.5%Approximately 0.5\%Approximately0.5%
B. Approximately9.1%Approximately 9.1\%Approximately9.1% ✓
C. Approximately8.7%Approximately 8.7\%Approximately8.7%
D. Approximately1.0%Approximately 1.0\%Approximately1.0%
Correct Answer: (B) — Approximately9.1%Approximately 9.1\%Approximately9.1%
Let D be the event that a person has the disease, and P be the event that the person tests positive.
We are given:
P(P|D) = 0.99 (True Positive Rate)
P(not P|not D) = 0.95 (True Negative Rate), which means P(P|not D) = 1 - 0.95 = 0.05 (False Positive Rate)
P(D) = 0.005 (Prevalence of the disease)
From P(D), we know P(not D) = 1 - P(D) = 1 - 0.005 = 0.995.
We want to find P(D|P), the probability that a person has the disease given a positive test result.
Using Bayes' Theorem:
P(D|P) = [P(P|D) * P(D)] / P(P)
To find P(P), we use the law of total probability:
P(P) = P(P|D) * P(D) + P(P|not D) * P(not D)
P(P) = (0.99 * 0.005) + (0.05 * 0.995)
P(P) = 0.00495 + 0.04975
P(P) = 0.0547
Now, substitute this back into Bayes' Theorem:
P(D|P) = (0.99 * 0.005) / 0.0547
P(D|P) = 0.00495 / 0.0547
P(D|P) \approx 0.09049
Converting to a percentage, this is approximately 9.05\%, which rounds to 9.1\%.
