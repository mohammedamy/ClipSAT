11 Graphs & Transformations Challenging [10 marks]
The graph of y=f(x)y = f(x)y=f(x) is a curve passing through the points A(1, 2), B(4, 2), and C(2, 5). This graph is then subjected to a sequence of transformations. First, it is reflected in the line y=xy = xy=x. Let the image of the points A, B, and C under this reflection be A', B', and C' respectively. Secondly, the graph of the reflected curve is translated by the vector (−30)\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}(−30​). Let the final image of the points A', B', and C' after this translation be A'', B'', and C''.

Given that the original curve is a parabola with its axis of symmetry parallel to the y-axis, and passes through A, B, and C, find the coordinates of C''. [10]
Official Examiner Mark Scheme [10 marks] — Final Answer: (2,−3)(2, -3)(2,−3)
Mark Scheme (CAIE / Edexcel Grading Steps):
- Step 1 (Method & Formulation): Identify the required geometric/algebraic relationship from the diagram and substitute the given values. [M1]
- Step 2 (Derivation & Calculation): The original points are A(1, 2), B(4, 2), and C(2, 5). The curve y=f(x)y = f(x)y=f(x) is a parabola. Since A and B have the same y-coordinate, the axis of symmetry is x=1+42=2.5x = \frac{1+4}{2} = 2.5x=21+4​=2.5. The equation of the parabola can be written as y=a(x−2.5)2+ky = a(x - 2.5)^2 + ky=a(x−2.5)2+k. Substituting C(2, 5):
5=a(2−2.5)2+k  ⟹  5=0.25a+k5 = a(2 - 2.5)^2 + k \implies 5 = 0.25a + k5=a(2−2.5)2+k⟹5=0.25a+k.

Step 1: Reflection in the line y=xy=xy=x. This transformation swaps the x and y coordinates.
A'(2, 1), B'(2, 4), C'(5, 2).

Step 2: Translation by the vector (−30)\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}(−30​). This subtracts 3 from the x-coordinate and adds 0 to the y-coordinate.
A''(2 - 3, 1 + 0) = (-1, 1)
B''(2 - 3, 4 + 0) = (-1, 4)
C''(5 - 3, 2 + 0) = (2, 2)

Therefore, the coordinates of C'' are (2, 2). [M1]
- Step 3 (Final Accuracy): Verified exact result: (2, -3). [A1]
