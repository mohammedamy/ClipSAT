OA⃗=(1 0 0)\vec{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \ 0 \end{pmatrix}OA=(1 0 0​), OB⃗=(0 1 0)\vec{OB} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix}OB=(0 1 0​), and OC⃗=(k k k2−5k+48)\vec{OC} = \begin{pmatrix} k \ k \ k^2 - 5k + 48 \end{pmatrix}OC=(k k k2−5k+48​).

det⁡(OA⃗,OB⃗,OC⃗)=∣10k 01k 00k2−5k+48∣\det(\vec{OA}, \vec{OB}, \vec{OC}) = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & k \ 0 & 1 & k \ 0 & 0 & k^2 - 5k + 48 \end{vmatrix}det(OA,OB,OC)=​1​0​k 0​1​k 0​0​k2−5k+48​​
Since this is an upper triangular matrix, the determinant is the product of its diagonal elements:
=1×1×(k2−5k+48)=k2−5k+48= 1 \times 1 \times (k^2 - 5k + 48) = k^2 - 5k + 48=1×1×(k2−5k+48)=k2−5k+48.

7=16∣k2−5k+48∣7 = \frac{1}{6} | k^2 - 5k + 48 |7=61​∣k2−5k+48∣
Multiplying both sides by 6:
42=∣k2−5k+48∣42 = | k^2 - 5k + 48 |42=∣k2−5k+48∣

This absolute value equation leads to two possible cases:

k2−5k+6=0k^2 - 5k + 6 = 0k2−5k+6=0

Case 2: k2−5k+48=−42k^2 - 5k + 48 = -42k2−5k+48=−42
