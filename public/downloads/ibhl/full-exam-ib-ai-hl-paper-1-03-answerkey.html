(a) Write down the key mathematical relationship or initial equation required to model this problem. [2 marks]
(b) Hence, or otherwise, find the exact required analytical solution, showing all working clearly. [2 marks]
Official Examiner Mark Scheme [4 marks] — Final Answer: 444
The x-intercepts of a function are the values of x for which f(x)=0f(x) = 0f(x)=0. For the quadratic function f(x)=x2−4x+3f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 3f(x)=x2−4x+3, we can find the x-intercepts by solving the equation x2−4x+3=0x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0x2−4x+3=0. This is a quadratic equation that can be factored. We are looking for two numbers that multiply to 3 and add to -4. These numbers are -1 and -3. Therefore, the equation can be factored as (x−1)(x−3)=0(x-1)(x-3) = 0(x−1)(x−3)=0. The solutions are x=1x=1x=1 and x=3x=3x=3. These are the x-intercepts. The sum of the x-intercepts is 1+3=41 + 3 = 41+3=4. Alternatively, for a quadratic equation of the form ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0, the sum of the roots (which correspond to the x-intercepts when f(x)=0f(x) = 0f(x)=0) is given by −ba-\frac{b}{a}−ab​. In this case, a=1a=1a=1 and b=−4b=-4b=−4, so the sum of the x-intercepts is −−41=4-\frac{-4}{1} = 4−1−4​=4. [M1] [A1]
