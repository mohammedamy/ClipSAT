The x-intercepts of a function are the values of x for which f(x)=0f(x) = 0f(x)=0. For the quadratic function f(x)=x2−4x+3f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 3f(x)=x2−4x+3, we can find the x-intercepts by solving the equation x2−4x+3=0x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0x2−4x+3=0. This is a quadratic equation that can be factored. We are looking for two numbers that multiply to 3 and add to -4. These numbers are -1 and -3. Therefore, the equation can be factored as (x−1)(x−3)=0(x-1)(x-3) = 0(x−1)(x−3)=0. The solutions are x=1x=1x=1 and x=3x=3x=3. These are the x-intercepts. The sum of the x-intercepts is 1+3=41 + 3 = 41+3=4.
