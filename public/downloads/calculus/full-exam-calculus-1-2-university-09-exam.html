34 Convergence Tests & Power Series Standard [1 mark]
Consider the power series ∑n=1∞(2x−1)nn⋅3n\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(2x-1)^n}{n \cdot 3^n}∑n=1∞​n⋅3n(2x−1)n​. For what values of xxx does this series converge conditionally?
A. −1<x<2-1 < x < 2−1<x<2
B. −1≤x<2-1 \le x < 2−1≤x<2
C. −1<x≤2-1 < x \le 2−1<x≤2
D. −1≤x≤2-1 \le x \le 2−1≤x≤2
Correct Answer: (C) — −1<x≤2-1 < x \le 2−1<x≤2
Let the given power series be ∑n=1∞an\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n∑n=1∞​an​, where an=(2x−1)nn⋅3na_n = \frac{(2x-1)^n}{n \cdot 3^n}an​=n⋅3n(2x−1)n​. We will use the Ratio Test to find the interval of convergence.

lim⁡n→∞∣an+1an∣=lim⁡n→∞∣(2x−1)n+1(n+1)⋅3n+1⋅n⋅3n(2x−1)n∣\lim_{n\to\infty} \left| \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} \right| = \lim_{n\to\infty} \left| \frac{(2x-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1) \cdot 3^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{n \cdot 3^n}{(2x-1)^n} \right|limn→∞​​an​an+1​​​=limn→∞​​(n+1)⋅3n+1(2x−1)n+1​⋅(2x−1)nn⋅3n​​

=lim⁡n→∞∣(2x−1)⋅n(n+1)⋅3∣=∣2x−13∣lim⁡n→∞nn+1=∣2x−13∣⋅1= \lim_{n\to\infty} \left| \frac{(2x-1) \cdot n}{(n+1) \cdot 3} \right| = \left| \frac{2x-1}{3} \right| \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{n}{n+1} = \left| \frac{2x-1}{3} \right| \cdot 1=limn→∞​​(n+1)⋅3(2x−1)⋅n​​=​32x−1​​limn→∞​n+1n​=​32x−1​​⋅1

For convergence, we require this limit to be less than 1:

∣2x−13∣<1\left| \frac{2x-1}{3} \right| < 1​32x−1​​<1

−1<2x−13<1-1 < \frac{2x-1}{3} < 1−1<32x−1​<1

−3<2x−1<3-3 < 2x-1 < 3−3<2x−1<3

−2<2x<4-2 < 2x < 4−2<2x<4

−1<x<2-1 < x < 2−1<x<2

Now, we must check the endpoints of this interval for conditional convergence.

Case 1: x=−1x = -1x=−1

The series becomes ∑n=1∞(2(−1)−1)nn⋅3n=∑n=1∞(−3)nn⋅3n=∑n=1∞(−1)nn\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(2(-1)-1)^n}{n \cdot 3^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-3)^n}{n \cdot 3^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}∑n=1∞​n⋅3n(2(−1)−1)n​=∑n=1∞​n⋅3n(−3)n​=∑n=1∞​n(−1)n​.

This is the alternating harmonic series, which converges conditionally by the Alternating Series Test (the terms 1n\frac{1}{n}n1​ are positive, decreasing, and tend to 0). Therefore, x=−1x=-1x=−1 is a point of conditional convergence.

Case 2: x=2x = 2x=2

The series becomes ∑n=1∞(2(2)−1)nn⋅3n=∑n=1∞(3)nn⋅3n=∑n=1∞1n\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(2(2)-1)^n}{n \cdot 3^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(3)^n}{n \cdot 3^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}∑n=1∞​n⋅3n(2(2)−1)n​=∑n=1∞​n⋅3n(3)n​=∑n=1∞​n1​.

This is the harmonic series, which diverges.

Thus, the series converges for −1≤x<2-1 \le x < 2−1≤x<2. For conditional convergence, we exclude the endpoint x=2x=2x=2 where the series diverges, and include x=−1x=-1x=−1 where it converges conditionally. So, the interval of conditional convergence is −1≤x<2-1 \le x < 2−1≤x<2.

Common Pitfall: Forgetting to test the endpoints for convergence, or misapplying convergence tests at the endpoints. Students might incorrectly assume convergence at x=2x=2x=2 or miss conditional convergence at x=−1x=-1x=−1.
