20 Transformations & Vectors Challenging [7 marks]
Triangle ABCABCABC has vertices A(−2,2)A(-2, 2)A(−2,2), B(1,3)B(1, 3)B(1,3), and C(−1,1)C(-1, 1)C(−1,1). First, △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC is reflected in the line y=xy = xy=x to give △A′B′C′\triangle A'B'C'△A′B′C′. Then △A′B′C′\triangle A'B'C'△A′B′C′ is translated by the vector (−31)\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}(−31​) to give △A′′B′′C′′\triangle A''B''C''△A′′B′′C′′. Find the coordinates of the vertices of △A′′B′′C′′\triangle A''B''C''△A′′B′′C′′. [7]
Official Examiner Mark Scheme [7 marks] — Final Answer: A''(-1, -1), B''(0, 2), C''(-2, 0)
Mark Scheme (CAIE / Edexcel Grading Steps):
- Step 1 (Method & Formulation): Identify the required geometric/algebraic relationship from the diagram and substitute the given values. [M1]
- Step 2 (Derivation & Calculation): Let the original triangle ABC have vertices A(-2, 2), B(1, 3), and C(-1, 1).

Step 1: Reflection in the line y=xy = xy=x.
When a point (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) is reflected in the line y=xy = xy=x, its image is (y,x)(y, x)(y,x).
Applying this to triangle ABC:
A(-2, 2) becomes A'(2, -2).
B(1, 3) becomes B'(3, 1).
C(-1, 1) becomes C'(1, -1).

Step 2: Translation of triangle A'B'C'.
Triangle A'B'C' is translated by the vector (−31)\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}(−31​). This means we subtract 3 from the x-coordinate and add 1 to the y-coordinate of each vertex.
Applying this to triangle A'B'C':
A'(2, -2) becomes A''(2 - 3, -2 + 1) = A''(-1, -1).
B'(3, 1) becomes B''(3 - 3, 1 + 1) = B''(0, 2).
C'(1, -1) becomes C''(1 - 3, -1 + 1) = C''(-2, 0).

Thus, the coordinates of the vertices of triangle A''B''C'' are A''(-1, -1), B''(0, 2), and C''(-2, 0). This matches choice A. [M1]
- Step 3 (Final Accuracy): Verified exact result: A''(-1, -1), B''(0, 2), C''(-2, 0). [A1]
