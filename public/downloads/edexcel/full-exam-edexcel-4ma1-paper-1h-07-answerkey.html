6 Algebra & Graphs Foundational [9 marks]
The graph of the quadratic function y=x2−4x+3y = x^2 - 4x + 3y=x2−4x+3 is shown. The graph of the linear function y=x−1y = x - 1y=x−1 is also shown. The graphs intersect at points A and B. Find the coordinates of point A.
Official Examiner Mark Scheme [9 marks] — Final Answer: (1,0)(1, 0)(1,0)
Mark Scheme (CAIE / Edexcel Grading Steps):
- Step 1 (Method & Formulation): Identify the required geometric/algebraic relationship from the diagram and substitute the given values. [M1]
- Step 2 (Derivation & Calculation): To find the intersection points, we set the two equations equal to each other: x2−4x+3=x−1x^2 - 4x + 3 = x - 1x2−4x+3=x−1. Rearranging the terms to form a quadratic equation: x2−5x+4=0x^2 - 5x + 4 = 0x2−5x+4=0. Factoring the quadratic equation: (x−1)(x−4)=0(x-1)(x-4) = 0(x−1)(x−4)=0. This gives us two possible x-values for the intersection points: x=1x = 1x=1 or x=4x = 4x=4.

For x=1x = 1x=1, we can find the corresponding y-coordinate using the linear equation y=x−1y = x - 1y=x−1: y=1−1=0y = 1 - 1 = 0y=1−1=0. So, one intersection point is (1,0)(1, 0)(1,0).

For x=4x = 4x=4, we can find the corresponding y-coordinate using the linear equation y=x−1y = x - 1y=x−1: y=4−1=3y = 4 - 1 = 3y=4−1=3. So, the other intersection point is (4,3)(4, 3)(4,3).

The question asks for the coordinates of point A. Looking at the provided graph, point A is the intersection point with the smaller x-coordinate. Therefore, point A has coordinates (1,0)(1, 0)(1,0). [M1]
- Step 3 (Final Accuracy): Verified exact result: (1, 0). [A1]
