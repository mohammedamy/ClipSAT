2 Geometry & Trigonometry Foundational [5 marks]
In the right-angled triangle ABC, shown below, AD is the altitude to the hypotenuse BC. If BD = 4 and DC = 9, find the length of the altitude AD.

(a) Write down the key mathematical relationship or initial equation required to model this problem. [2 marks]
(b) Hence, or otherwise, find the exact required analytical solution, showing all working clearly. [3 marks]
Official Examiner Mark Scheme [5 marks] — Final Answer: 666
In a right-angled triangle, the altitude to the hypotenuse creates similar triangles. By the Geometric Mean Theorem, the square of the altitude to the hypotenuse is equal to the product of the lengths of the two segments it divides the hypotenuse into. Thus, AD2=BD×DCAD^2 = BD \times DCAD2=BD×DC. Given BD=4BD = 4BD=4 and DC=9DC = 9DC=9, we have AD2=4×9=36AD^2 = 4 \times 9 = 36AD2=4×9=36. Taking the square root of both sides, we get AD=36=6AD = \sqrt{36} = 6AD=36​=6. [M1] [A1]
